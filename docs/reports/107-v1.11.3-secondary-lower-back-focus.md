# v1.11.3 — Optional secondary lower-back focus

## Task and requirements

Task 06 (`GEN-003`, `GEN-007`, `GEN-011`, `GEN-015`) and `AT-M05`. Owner request on 2026-09-15: permit exercises where lower back is meaningfully involved but is not the source primary target. This task is stacked on still-open PR #27, itself stacked on PR #26; no earlier generic Go approves either merge.

## Audit and decision

The reviewed local catalogue has 307 records, including five with primary lower back. Twenty-one reviewed records list lower back as a secondary muscle; the majority are squats, abdominal movements, side laterals or highly incidental stabilization. Simply allowing every secondary tag would reproduce the misclassification the prior owner report sought to correct. The pinned source already contains paired local photos for the three retained options; no new source or binary media is needed.

Only three existing, distinct setups are admitted to a separate opt-in pool: **Romanian Deadlift** (barbell bilateral hip hinge; source primary hamstrings), **Kettlebell One-Legged Deadlift** (unilateral balance/hinge; source primary hamstrings), and **Bent Over One-Arm Long Bar Row** (unsupported bent-over pull; source primary middle back). In each case the pinned source tags lower back secondarily; the app does not relabel it as primary. ACE describes the Romanian deadlift as posterior-chain training including spinal erectors, NSCA describes erector involvement in unilateral RDL-type hinges, and ACE-sponsored back exercise research reports erector activation in bent-over rows. The one-arm long-bar row application is a reviewed inference from that row category, not a claim that ACE tested that exact variant. No treatment or safety claim is made.

Sources: [Free Exercise DB pinned-source repository](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db), [ACE Romanian deadlift technique](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/january-2016/5767/ace-technique-series-romanian-deadlift/), [NSCA unilateral hinge discussion](https://www.nsca.com/contentassets/9dab08fc28274f3ba1d76af31bbbe40b/coach3.1-screening_and_preventing_common_injuries_in_division_i_basketball_players.pdf), [ACE-sponsored bent-over row research](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/special-dec-2018-issue/7146/ace-sponsored-research-what-is-the-best-back-exercise/).

## Before and after

Previously Lower back was intentionally strict (primary target only, 15–30 minutes); searching the library with secondary muscles enabled showed all twenty-one tags, but the generator offered no honest way to combine direct and supporting movements. Now the quick-session Body area menu adds **Lower back + supporting work** as a separate stable zone ID, leaving existing `lower-back` and saved sessions unchanged. The mixed choice allows 15–45 minutes including programmed recovery and requires at least one direct and one reviewed secondary exercise. Each preview/alternative/current/upcoming exercise is labelled Direct lower back or Lower back secondary; the active workout displays the readable zone label instead of a raw ID.

To avoid junk volume, a mixed plan uses no more than one reviewed back-extension variant and no more than one of Barbell Deadlift/Romanian Deadlift. A 50–60 minute mixed session could not satisfy the documented 90–110% duration range without violating those caps; the UI disables those durations and the domain validator returns explicit Full back guidance. The original strict Lower back choice remains 15–30 minutes. No warming-up content was added, and recovery remains user-selected and counted in the estimate.

The existing central eligibility gate still rejects unavailable apparatus, hard exclusions and Never Suggest before scoring. Generation fails if direct or reviewed secondary options are unavailable. Preview and active-session alternatives will not replace the last remaining Direct with Secondary or vice versa. Saved-program editing remains user-controlled; it does not rewrite historical sets.
